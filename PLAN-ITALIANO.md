## Rules

1. **Pimsleur is strictly sequential.** The app picks the lesson by date. If
   you are behind, do the lesson you are actually on, not the one the date
   says — skipping Pimsleur lessons breaks it. Tick the day anyway; the
   checkbox means "did the audio task," not "did that exact lesson."
2. **Anki due cards before new cards**, always.
3. **Subtitles in Italian, never Spanish** (phase 2 onward).
4. **Minimum day** when it collapses: Anki reviews only (5–10 min). Keeps the
   deck alive; doesn't count as a full day.
5. **Two missed days in a row** → next session is reviews + one Pimsleur
   lesson only, no new Anki cards, to rebuild the habit before the load.

## Proposed additions (not in the app yet — say which you want)

These fill gaps in the app rather than adding volume.

**A. Start speaking now, not on day 121 (strongest recommendation).** The app
only adds conversation in phase 3. You have something most learners pay a
tutor for — each other. From now: *cena in italiano*, 10 min, 2×/week.
Rule: no switching to Spanish; if you don't know the word, describe it.

**B. Put the "lettura" into phase 2.** Phase 2 is named "Espansione e
lettura," but none of its daily tasks is reading. The app already links four
reading PDFs (Letture I · 1–2, Letture III · 1–2). Proposal: read one aloud
to each other, 2×/week, swapped in for that day's immersion task.

**C. A Spanish → Italian trap list.** For Spanish speakers, Italian is
unusually close (~80% shared vocabulary), so the risk is *interference*,
not difficulty. Worth one Anki tag of its own:

| Italian | Means | Not |
|---|---|---|
| burro | mantequilla | burro (= asino) |
| salire | subir | salir (= uscire) |
| guardare | mirar | guardar (= conservare) |
| caldo | caliente | caldo (= brodo) |
| largo | ancho | largo (= lungo) |
| camera | cuarto / habitación | cámara |
| subito | enseguida | súbito |
| gamba | pierna | — |
| topo | ratón | tope |

Grammar traps: **double consonants change meaning** (*pena / penna*, *caro /
carro*); **passato prossimo takes *essere* with motion/change verbs**
(*sono andato*, not *ho andato* — Spanish always uses *haber*); articles
**il / lo / l' / i / gli / le**; the particles **ci** and **ne**, which
Spanish doesn't have.

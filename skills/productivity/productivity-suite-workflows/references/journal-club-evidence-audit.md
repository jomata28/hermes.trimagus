# Evidence audit for journal-club decks

Use this checklist for original research when the deck must support live scientific questioning, not only summarize the abstract.

## Source hierarchy

1. Final article and figure captions
2. Supplementary methods and extended data
3. Reporting summary or protocol
4. Source-data workbooks and deposited datasets
5. Peer-review history, used only to identify criticisms, revisions, or unresolved design concerns

If sources conflict, the final published article and final source data govern. Do not let an earlier reviewer response override the final paper.

## Design facts to extract

- Species or population, sex, strain, age, setting, and eligibility
- Intervention dose, route, schedule, comparator, and treatment duration
- Separate cohorts and which outcomes belong to each cohort
- Randomization, masking, exclusions, censoring, humane endpoints, and replication
- Exact sample sizes per assay, not only the headline cohort size
- Prespecified versus exploratory outcomes
- Statistical directionality, multiplicity correction, repeated-measures model, and significance threshold
- Funding, patents, author conflicts, and data availability

## Claim-strength ladder

For each slide and speaker paragraph, classify the claim:

1. **Demonstrated:** direct endpoint with appropriate comparison
2. **Supported:** convergent but indirect evidence
3. **Associated:** biomarker, pathway enrichment, or correlation
4. **Proposed:** mechanism or translation not causally tested

Use language that matches the level. Avoid converting association into mechanism or a single-model animal result into a human treatment claim.

## Comparator audit

Ask whether the experiment includes the control needed for the spoken claim:

- Untreated control versus vehicle or procedure control
- Pair-fed or exposure-matched control
- Young reference group for restoration or rejuvenation claims
- Active comparator for superiority claims
- Baseline measurements for claims of slowed decline
- Causal perturbation or rescue for pathway necessity

If a control is absent, state exactly what cannot be separated.

## Figure manifest before layout

For every slide, record:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Slide claim | One sentence the audience should remember |
| Primary panel group | Minimum paper panels that directly prove the claim |
| Required labels | Panel letters, axes, legends, P values, scale bars, and group labels |
| Spoken context | Methods or caveats moved into the script |
| Exclusions | Redundant or too-dense panels deliberately omitted |

Default to one primary visual group per result slide. Use more than one only when the slide explicitly compares orthogonal domains and each remains legible on projection.

## Script synchronization

After any visual simplification pass, reread the exact script. Replace phrases such as “the upper panels” or “shown here” if those panels were removed or moved to another slide. The script must describe the final deck, not an earlier draft.

## Assay and statistics concept readiness

After writing the slide script, identify specialized terms visible on selected panels or used in the narration. Prepare short plain-language explanations so the presenter is ready for follow-up questions. Prioritize:

- Statistical adjustments such as ANCOVA: name the outcome, group comparison, covariate, why adjustment was needed, what persistence after adjustment supports, and what it still cannot prove.
- Labeling assays such as BrdU: explain what incorporation directly measures and why it does not by itself establish mature, integrated, functional cells.
- Differentiation markers such as DCX: state the cell stage represented and pair it with proliferation evidence rather than treating it as interchangeable.
- Ageing paradoxes such as expanded phenotypic HSC pools with impaired function: explain why a lower marker-defined count can coexist with improved colony output or lineage balance.
- Surrogate molecular markers: distinguish transcript abundance, protein abundance, pathway activity, and causal necessity.

Add these explanations to the script question bank or presenter notes when they are likely to be asked. Do not crowd the slide with definitions unless understanding the main result requires them.

## Final challenge questions

Prepare short answers for:

- Could the primary result be explained by the comparator or a missing control?
- Is the result longitudinal, cross-sectional, or both?
- Which outcomes remained significant after adjustment?
- Were one-sided tests or permissive thresholds used?
- Are sample sizes consistent across assays?
- Is the mechanism causal or associative?
- What is the strongest defensible translational statement?
- What conflict or patent disclosure should be mentioned?
